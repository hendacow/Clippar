# Clippar — Trade Mark Filing Prep (Australia)

**Prepared 2026-08-04. Not legal advice** — this is search results plus a filing plan.
For a mark with a phonetic-neighbour risk like this one, a 30-minute paid consult with a
trade marks attorney before filing is cheap insurance.

---

## 1. What the search found

Source: Australian Trade Mark Search (search.ipaustralia.gov.au), searched 2026-08-04.

| Search | Result |
|---|---|
| `clippar` — exact, all classes, all statuses | **0 results** |
| `clipper` — all classes | 313 total · 98 registered · 107 removed · 89 never registered |
| `clipper` — class 9 + associated (9, 11, 14, 16, 28, 37, 38, 41, 42, 45) | 50 results, **almost all Removed / Lapsed / Never registered** |

**The one live mark found in the class-9 associated set:** `CLIPPERMAN` (TM 878415),
classes 8 and 37, registered — a hair-clipper/barbering mark. Not a realistic conflict
with golf video software.

Live `CLIPPER` registrations that do exist sit in unrelated classes — 25 (clothing),
8 (hand tools), 34 (tobacco), 16 (paper). None of those are your classes.

### What this means
- **Good news:** the exact word "Clippar" is unclaimed in Australia, and your target
  classes look effectively clear of live conflicts.
- **The real risk is not exact-match, it's s44 "deceptive similarity."** "Clippar" is
  phonetically identical to "clipper", which is a dictionary word with many registrations.
  An examiner *could* raise an objection. The search suggests they shouldn't, because the
  live clipper marks are in unrelated classes — but this is exactly the judgement call an
  examiner makes and I can't make for you.
- **Mitigation:** file via TM Headstart (below), which buys you an examiner's opinion
  *before* you commit the full fee.

## 2. Do NOT file in all 45 classes

Asked for "as many sections as possible" — here is why that would be a bad decision:

- **Cost.** 45 classes × $250 = **$11,250**, or ~$14,850 via Headstart. That is most or all
  of your $15k investment, spent on protection you cannot use.
- **You must intend to use each class.** Under the Trade Marks Act you declare an intention
  to use the mark for the goods/services claimed. Claiming 45 classes when you sell an app
  and a phone mount is not sustainable.
- **Non-use removal.** After 3 years, anyone can apply to strip classes you aren't trading
  in. You would lose them anyway, having paid for them.
- **Bad faith risk.** Wildly over-broad claims can be challenged on that basis.

**Correct strategy: 2 classes now, add later if the business expands.** Trade marks can be
added to; you file a fresh application for a new class when you actually enter it.

## 3. Recommended classes

### Class 9 — PRIMARY, file this one
Covers the app *and* the hardware in one class.
- Downloadable mobile applications for recording and editing video
- Downloadable computer software for video capture, editing and analysis
- Mounts and holders for smartphones
- Wireless remote controls for cameras / electronic devices
- Battery chargers *(only if you keep the Premium kit's MagSafe pad)*

### Class 42 — SECONDARY, recommended
Covers the cloud/subscription side that class 9 doesn't reach.
- Software as a service (SaaS) featuring software for video editing
- Providing temporary use of non-downloadable software for video processing
- Cloud computing services for video rendering

### Optional, only if you'll genuinely trade there
- **Class 41** — entertainment services; providing online video content. Worth it only if
  you start publishing Clippar-branded content as a product.
- **Class 35** — online retail store services. Usually *not* needed when you're selling your
  own goods under your own mark; this class is for retailing others' goods.
- **Class 25** — clothing. Only if merch becomes real.
- **Class 28** — sporting goods. A buggy phone mount is class 9, not 28. Skip.

## 4. Route and cost

Two ways to file. For Clippar, **TM Headstart is the right one** because of the
clipper-similarity question — it gets an examiner to look before you're committed.

| Route | Cost per class | Total for 2 classes | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TM Headstart** | $200 assessment + $130 to convert = **$330** | **$660** | Examiner pre-assessment in ~5 business days. If they flag a problem you can amend or walk away, having spent only the $200/class. |
| Standard application (picklist) | **$250** | **$500** | Straight to filing. Cheaper, but if an objection comes back you've spent the money and must fight it. |

Saving $160 is not worth flying blind on a mark that sounds exactly like a dictionary word.

**Use the picklist.** Free-text descriptions cost $400/class instead of $250 and slow
examination down. The picklist has 60,000+ pre-approved terms; everything listed in §3
above exists in it.

Other things to budget for:
- Registration fee: nil at present for standard applications (included).
- Renewal: due 10 years from the filing date.
- Total timeline: ~7–8 months to registration if unopposed.

## 5. Going global

Do **not** file internationally yet. The sequence that saves money:

1. File in Australia first. Your filing date becomes your **priority date**.
2. You then have **6 months** to file in other countries and claim that same priority date,
   via the Madrid Protocol (one WIPO application, pick your countries).
3. Only pay for countries you'll actually trade in. On current plans that's the **US** —
   25M golfers, and it's where the model says the business has to work.

Filing Madrid now, before you know whether the US is real, is the same mistake as filing
45 classes.

**Before the US:** search USPTO TESS for "clippar" and "clipper" in class 9. The US has a
much denser trade mark register than Australia and a genuine "Clipper" conflict is more
likely there.

## 6. What you need to have ready to file

Have these to hand and the form takes about 20 minutes:

- [ ] **Applicant name** — decide now: you personally, or a company? If you intend to raise
      further investment, file in the **company's** name, not your own. Moving a mark later
      is an assignment with paperwork and fees. Check with the accelerator whether a company
      exists yet.
- [ ] **ABN/ACN** if filing as a company
- [ ] **Address for service** in Australia
- [ ] **The mark** — decide whether you're filing:
      - the **word** "Clippar" (broadest protection, recommended — covers any styling), or
      - the **logo** (narrower, protects that specific device only)
      - Best practice: word mark first. Logo later if the branding settles.
- [ ] **Classes** — 9 and 42 per §3
- [ ] **Goods/services** — picked from the picklist, per §3
- [ ] **Payment card** — you must enter this yourself
- [ ] **IP Australia online services account** — you must create this yourself

## 7. Also worth locking down while you're at it

- **ASIC business name** — check "Clippar" is registered to you at connectonline.asic.gov.au
- **Domains** — you have clippargolf.com. Consider clippar.com.au (requires an ABN) and
  defensive registration of clippar.app
- **App Store name** — reserve "Clippar" in App Store Connect as part of the launch. A
  registered trade mark also helps if you ever need to file an App Store dispute against a
  copycat.
- **Social handles** — @clippar.golf is referenced in the pitch material; lock the rest.
